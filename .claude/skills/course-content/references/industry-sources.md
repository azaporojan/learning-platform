# Industry sources to cite

Cite the primary standard or official documentation, never a tutorial blog, when a slide or card
states a definition, a rule or a number. Tutorials (guru99, tutorialspoint, educative) can be listed
as "further reading" only.

**Before a link goes into a deck, verify it** (WebFetch it, or WebSearch for it when the sandbox
proxy blocks the host) and check that the page says what the slide claims. Exact section numbers and
versions matter: ISTQB CTFL is v4.0 (current minor revision v4.0.1, 2024); quote "v4.0" unless the
point depends on the revision.

## Quotes

Use a quote only when you can name its author, the work, the year, and a link where a reader can
check the wording. Copy the wording exactly. Good, checkable examples:

- "Program testing can be used to show the presence of bugs, but never to show their absence!" —
  Edsger W. Dijkstra, *Notes on Structured Programming* (EWD249), 1970.
  https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD02xx/EWD249/EWD249.html
- Syllabus sentences are the safest quotes: e.g. ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.2.2 "Testing is a form of quality
  control (QC)." Quote, then give the section number.

Never attribute a popular "quote" you cannot source (many circulating testing quotes are misattributed).
